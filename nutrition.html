Essential Nutrients

𑣲⋆ Protein, Fat, and Carbohydrates: These nutrients provide energy and support growth, tissue repair, and normal body functions. The right balance depends on your conure's individual needs and overall diet.

𑣲⋆ Vitamins and Minerals: Vitamin A supports vision, skin, feathers, and immune function. Calcium supports strong bones and normal muscle function.

𑣲⋆ Fibre: Fibre from vegetables and other suitable whole foods helps support normal digestive function.

𑣲⋆ Healthy Fats: Fats provide energy and help the body absorb certain vitamins. Seeds and nuts should be offered in appropriate, limited portions.

𑣲⋆ Water: Always provide clean, fresh drinking water. Change it at least daily and sooner if it becomes dirty.

Important: A balanced, high-quality formulated pellet diet helps provide essential nutrients. Do not add vitamin or calcium supplements unless an avian veterinarian recommends them.

Healthy Vegetables

☆ Broccoli: Wash thoroughly and serve raw or plain cooked.

☆ Carrots: Wash and serve finely chopped, grated, or lightly steamed.

☆ Kale: Wash thoroughly and serve in manageable pieces as part of a varied diet.

☆ Bell Peppers: Wash, remove the stem, and cut into small pieces.

☆ Peas: Offer fresh or plain cooked peas that have cooled before serving.

☆ Zucchini: Wash and chop into small pieces. Serve raw or plain cooked.

☆ Sweet Potato: Cook thoroughly until soft and allow it to cool before serving.

Tip: Offer a variety of vegetables and introduce unfamiliar foods gradually. Never add salt, butter, oil, or seasoning.

❌ Dangerous Foods ❌

❌ Avocado: Contains compounds that can be toxic to birds.

❌ Chocolate and Cocoa: Can cause serious poisoning.

❌ Caffeine: Coffee, tea, energy drinks, and other caffeinated products can be dangerous.

❌ Alcohol: Toxic to birds, even in small amounts.

❌ Onions and Garlic: Avoid feeding these foods to your conure.

❌ Apple Seeds and Fruit Pits: Remove apple seeds and pits from fruits such as cherries, peaches, and plums before serving.

❌ Raw Dried Beans: Uncooked dried beans can contain dangerous toxins. Never feed them to your bird.

❌ Rhubarb: Avoid feeding rhubarb to your conure.

❌ Moldy or Spoiled Food: Discard spoiled produce and food that may contain harmful mold.

Safety Reminder: Never offer unfamiliar human foods without checking whether they are safe for parrots. If your conure eats a potentially toxic food, contact an avian veterinarian immediately.

Food Intolerances & Warning Signs

Digestive Changes: Watch for unusual vomiting, repeated regurgitation, changes in droppings, reduced appetite, or digestive problems that persist.

Skin and Feathers: Repeated scratching, skin irritation, unusual feather damage, or excessive feather plucking may indicate an underlying problem.

Monitor New Foods: Introduce new foods gradually and observe your bird's appetite, droppings, and behaviour. Keep track of foods that appear to cause repeated problems.

Important: These symptoms do not necessarily indicate a food allergy or intolerance. An avian veterinarian can help identify the cause.

Emergency Warning: Difficulty breathing, open-mouth breathing, sudden facial swelling, collapse, or severe weakness requires immediate veterinary attention.

Building a Balanced Conure Diet

☆ High-Quality Formulated Pellets: Provide essential nutrients and form the nutritional foundation of most pet conure diets.

☆ Fresh Vegetables: Offer a variety of washed vegetables regularly to provide important vitamins, minerals, and fibre.

☆ Fresh Fruits: Offer small portions in moderation because fruits contain natural sugars.

☆ Seeds and Nuts: Offer appropriate varieties in limited quantities. They can be high in fat and should not replace a balanced diet.

☆ Suitable Protein Sources: A balanced formulated pellet diet generally provides protein. Other suitable foods may be offered in moderation when appropriate.

☆ Fresh Drinking Water: Always provide clean water and refresh it regularly throughout the day.

Remember: Every conure has individual nutritional needs. Age, activity, body condition, and health can affect dietary requirements. Consult an avian veterinarian before making major dietary changes or adding supplements.
